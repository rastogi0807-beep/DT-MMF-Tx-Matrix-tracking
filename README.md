# DT-MMF-Tx-Matrix-tracking
A self-calibrating physics-informed digital twin is proposed for multimode-fiber transmission-matrix tracking using active pilot selection, receiver calibration, and failure detection. Simulations on 24 independent trajectories show a 29.30% reduction in TM error, 95.45% uncertainty coverage, and 99.66% nominal availability.

The central test used 24 independent fibers/trajectories, seven scenarios, 160 frames and five main methods, with two ablations in three scenarios. Sensitivity tests used 12 new seeds and six settings. Weaker mismatch tests used eight new seeds at six severities. The six tuning and ten uncertainty-calibration seeds are disjoint from all test seeds. On the recorded execution host, the main test took about 669 seconds and the sensitivity test about 279 seconds. These are execution-host wall times, not optical acquisition latencies or real-time hardware claims.

The method assumes known initial bases, singular values and six perturbation generators, coherent field access and a stable shared calibration reference. Its posterior omits uncertainty in commissioning. It is a reduced coupled-mode proof of concept, not a full Maxwell simulation, a neural PINN, a blind full-TM recovery method, a distal-free endoscope, or an experimentally validated digital twin. Persistent family mismatch is withheld, not automatically repaired. The passive counterexample demonstrates that four current inputs leave a large blind subspace for arbitrary changes.
